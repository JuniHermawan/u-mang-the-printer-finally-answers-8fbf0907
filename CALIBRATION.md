# Calibration — "The Printer Finally Answers"

Judge brief (house judge, pairwise, in order): (1) the line is the office
printer's own comeback to whoever jams it, thumps it and stares at its
cryptic error codes — another voice loses to any line in the printer's own
voice; (2) funny — the laugh arrives immediately, a real joke, not a
complaint with a punchline bolted on; (3) sounds like the machine itself —
weary, literal, faintly passive-aggressive, flat logic of a device that has
counted every sheet it ever fed; (4) lands in a single read, no setup, no
second sentence, no knowledge beyond the office; (5) fresh — not the same
paper-jam line recycled at every office party. Length for its own sake
does not count.

Rubric: each variant scored 1–5 on Voice / Funny / Machine tone /
Landing / Freshness (max 25), in the judge's criterion order.

| V7 | Error code 0xE3. Translation: you put the paper in crooked, then hit me. I am a printer, not a pinball machine. (111) | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 20 |

Notes: V4 dies on freshness — PC LOAD LETTER is the recycled office-party
joke the brief explicitly penalises. V1/V5 front-load an error code and the
joke arrives late. V3 is punchy but the vending-machine comparison is not
printer logic. V6 is strong but "Coincidence?" is a weaker punchline than a
threat. V2 wins: first-person printer, a real count, and a threat that is
itself a printer act (printing the resignation letter in Comic Sans).

## Head-to-head (pairwise, winner in bold)

- V2 vs V3 → **V2** (sharper joke; counting is printer logic, comparison is not)
- V2 vs V6 → **V2** (Comic Sans threat out-punches the rhetorical question)
- V2 vs V7 → **V2** (threat lands harder than "not a pinball machine")
- V2 vs V1 → **V2** (joke arrives immediately, no error-code throat-clearing)
- V2 vs V4 → **V2** (fresh vs recycled)
- V2 vs V5 → **V2** (one threat vs a diffuse list)
- V3 vs V6 → **V6** (machine counting beats the comparison)

Winner of round 1: **V2** (23/25).

## Round 2 — revise the winner (V2 → v5)

- v3 (113): "I have fed 41,209 sheets. Not one thank-you. Jam the tray again
  and your resignation letter prints in Comic Sans." — "fed" is the printer's
  own verb; tighter clauses. Score 5/5/5/4/4 = 23.
- v4 (124): "I have fed 41,209 sheets without a single thank-you. Jam the
  tray again and your resignation letter comes out in Comic Sans." —
  "comes out" is printer language, but wordier. Score 5/5/5/4/4 = 23.
- v5 (111): "I have fed 41,209 sheets. Zero thank-yous. Jam the tray again
  and your resignation letter prints in Comic Sans." — "Zero thank-yous" is
  the most machine-literal beat (a count, again), shortest clauses, best
  sticker rhythm. Score 5/5/5/5/4 = **24**.

Head-to-head: v5 beats v3 (punchier middle beat), v5 beats v4 (tighter
landing), v3 beats v4 (shorter, same joke).
